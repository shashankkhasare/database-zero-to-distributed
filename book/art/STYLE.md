# Book Illustration Direction

The book uses original editorial illustrations to make database ideas tangible
and to give long technical chapters moments of visual rest. Conceptual artwork
is generated as raster imagery; exact plans, byte layouts, and data-flow
diagrams remain reproducible SVG or HTML assets.

## Reusable style prompt

```text
Original editorial illustration for a technical book about building a database.
Hand-drawn ink contours with restrained gouache and watercolor fills on lightly
textured warm paper. Friendly, intelligent, and slightly playful, but never
childish. Simplified geometric forms, confident imperfect lines, subtle print
texture, generous negative space, and one immediately readable visual metaphor.
Palette: warm ivory paper, charcoal ink, deep database teal, muted turquoise,
ochre gold, and very small rust-red accents. Landscape composition suitable for
a book chapter. No photorealism, no glossy 3D rendering, no gradients that look
digital, no interface chrome, no logos, no watermark, and no written words.
Create an original visual identity. Do not imitate any named artist, illustrator,
book, studio, or publication.
```

## Consistency rules

- Use the same paper, ink, teal, turquoise, and ochre family across chapters.
- Give each picture one main metaphor that remains clear at column width.
- Keep faces and figures simple and inclusive rather than portrait-like.
- Leave technical labels to adjacent HTML or SVG whenever possible.
- Do not embed prose, code, SQL, or small labels in generated raster artwork.
- Store final images under `book/images/` and record their prompts and provenance.
